AI for Sales Operations in Consumer Goods

16 October 2026  ·  9 minute read  ·  Adopt AI Partners

A remittance advice arrives from a grocer with eleven deduction lines against one week of invoices. Two quote a code that means nothing outside that retailer's finance department. One is a penalty for a March delivery that went short. The rest say only "deduction" and an amount. The money is already withheld. Somebody has to decide, inside the query window the trading agreement allows, which lines to accept and which to challenge, and then produce, for each challenge, the signed promotional agreement, the price that applied on the day and the proof of delivery.

That is sales operations in consumer goods. Not pipeline hygiene, not a weekly forecast call. It is the administration of the distance between your list price and what a handful of very large customers eventually pay.

The money sits in the gap between list price and what the grocer pays

Trade spend is usually the second largest line after the goods themselves, and it is not fixed by a factory. It is the sum of hundreds of separate commitments: an off invoice discount, a retrospective rebate, a bonus that pays only if volume passes a threshold nobody can settle until February. Each is negotiated by one account manager, recorded imperfectly, and claimed back at the customer's convenience.

So the job is reconciliation and evidence against a commercial clock. Did we agree this. What shipped. What did they sell. Does the claim match any of it, and if not, what can we show. Little of that is judgement and most of it is assembling documents that sit in different systems and arrive in different shapes, which is good ground for agents: the hard part is not deciding, it is reading enough fast enough for a person to decide.

Signing the joint business plan is the easy part

A joint business plan with a major grocer is negotiated in a handful of meetings and then governs a year: volume commitments, an agreed promotional calendar, an investment envelope, distribution targets. The negotiation is the part everybody remembers; the year that follows is the part nobody owns. An agent can turn the signed plan into a register, one row per commitment, with the obligation, the date and the clause it came from, then say in week thirty what has been met, what is drifting, and what the customer is about to invoice you for.

The annual terms are agreed once and referenced all year

The trading terms document sits underneath everything else. Settlement discount, distribution allowance, logistics income, a marketing contribution expressed as a percentage of something, growth bonus thresholds, payment days, the penalty regime for short deliveries. It is signed once, then lives in somebody's mailbox while every claim for twelve months is tested against it from memory. Structuring it suits an agent: each term becomes a field with its value, its basis, its period and a pointer back to the clause, so the register answers the question actually asked, whether the rate on this claim is the rate we signed.

Every price change downstream is a volume change upstream

Promotional planning lives in a spreadsheet, one per customer with a tab per quarter: mechanic, period, participating lines, funded depth, who pays which part, expected uplift, the baseline. That plan has a second life the moment it is agreed, because a deep cut on a core line for a fortnight is a manufacturing instruction, a haulage booking and a raw material call.

Keeping the calendar and the forecast in the same world

An agent can read the calendar as it stands today, derive the uplift profile per line per week, and compare it against what the demand plan assumes. The useful output is the disagreements: promotions with no uplift loaded, forecast spikes with nothing behind them, two mechanics on one line in the same week, a promotion agreed after the supply cut off.

An accrual is a position you will have to defend

You accrue at the agreed rate against volume that shipped on deal, and the gap between planned, accrued, claimed and settled spend is the number finance asks about at every close. It moves because promotions get extended, mechanics get changed on a call, and the deal a line shipped on is not always the deal planned for it. An agent can hold those four numbers side by side per promotion, age the open accrual, and list the promotions where it has sat untouched long after a claim should have arrived.

Nobody finishes the post event evaluation

Few evaluations are finished, because the sell out data arrives weeks later, the baseline is contested, the costs are not settled until the deduction lands, and by then the team is negotiating the next quarter. So the same mechanic runs again on the strength of having run before. Given the agreement, the shipped volume, the scan data and the settled spend, an agent can assemble the pack to a method you fix in advance and compare it against the last three times this mechanic ran on this line.

Eight months later, a deduction appears on the remittance

A claim arrives long after the activity, as a line with a reference number, an amount and no supporting document. Resolving it means working backwards: which promotion is this reference, was there a signed agreement, which lines were in scope, and does the amount withheld relate to the answer. At volume that becomes triage by instinct, which is how companies write off claims they could have disproved. An agent can match each line against the promotional register and the shipment history, clear what reconciles inside a tolerance you set, and prepare the rest as a packet: the claim, the agreement it refers to, the volume underneath it, and why the match failed.

Two parties count the same volume and reach different answers

Listing fees, retrospective rebates and growth bonuses all settle against a volume, and the volume depends on which definition the terms used. Your despatched cases, their received cases, their scanned units, net of returns or not, by financial calendar or by promotional period. Reasonable people reading one clause reach different totals, and the difference is real money that surfaces once a year with a tight response time. An agent can compute the figure on each defensible basis, name the clause that governs, and isolate the weeks and lines where the two sides diverge, rather than leaving an account manager to argue about an aggregate they cannot break down.

Sell in and sell out rarely describe the same week

What you invoiced and what the shopper bought are different businesses, and the gap is stock sitting in a depot, a wholesaler's shed or on a distributor's pallet. Sell out reporting is the only corrective, and it comes from the customer, so it arrives in whatever shape their systems produce: a weekly extract with merged headers, a portal download keyed on their own product codes, a distributor file with a different column order from last month. An agent handles this as interpretation rather than transformation, and what matters is the rows it could not resolve, carried forward with their raw values and a reason rather than dropped so the totals look tidy.

A photograph from a store aisle is evidence or it is nothing

Field sales and merchandising produce the only direct proof that what was paid for happened. A gondola end was built, the secondary display was sited, the point of sale material went up. Those observations sit as photographs and short forms in a field app, tagged with a store, a date and a person, and are almost never connected to the promotion that paid for them. When a retailer claims the activity underperformed, that connection is the argument, so an agent can collate visit evidence against each promotion and store list, report compliance as a rate with the gaps named, and attach the images to a dispute packet.

Getting a new line onto the system before the launch date

New line forms and range review submissions are a deadline with no flexibility in them. The retailer wants its own template or its own portal, populated with case dimensions, pallet configuration, barcodes, shelf life, nutritional values, allergen declarations, country of origin, artwork and a volume forecast, with validation that rejects the whole file over one malformed field.

Drafting from what you already hold

Almost every value exists somewhere: a specification document, a product data system, a packaging artwork file, a previous submission to a different grocer. An agent can assemble the draft from those sources, mark each field with where it came from, and flag the ones it could not source or where two systems disagree. A conflict between the specification and the artwork is worth finding before a buyer finds it, and a person still submits, because the declared values are a regulatory statement.

Where AI fails here

The negotiation is not available for automation. What a buyer will accept, what you can concede and what the relationship will bear are judgements made by the people who carry the account.

Writing off a deduction is a commercial decision, not a matching result. Plenty of unsupported claims get accepted anyway, because the amount is small, the customer is large and the next range review is in six weeks. An agent set to dispute everything that fails to reconcile will be right and will spend goodwill you cannot buy back.

Promotional baselines are contested by construction, and an agent reporting incrementality to two decimal places dresses an assumption as a measurement.

Data wrong at source cannot be repaired downstream. If the sell out file reports a depot rather than a store, nothing recovers where the product went.

Retailer portals change without notice, so treat submission into them as assisted, not automated. And all of this produces an exception queue by design. If nobody owns that queue weekly, exceptions pile up until someone clears them in bulk under deadline pressure, which is the practice you were replacing.

Where to start

Start with deduction matching for a single retailer. The ground truth is unambiguous, because a claim either reconciles to a signed agreement and a shipped volume or it does not, the output is a prepared packet rather than a payment, and the backlog is already late and already costing money.

Leave the rest alone at first. Do not touch the joint business plan negotiation, do not let anything decide promotional investment, and do not give an agent write access to a retailer portal. Build the trading terms register and the promotional agreement register first, because everything else here depends on them.

Then replay a closed period, feeding the agent the remittances and agreements as they arrived and comparing its conclusions against what you accepted and disputed. Agree the acceptance bar before anyone looks at the results, and write down which decisions always carry a human name: accepting a claim, raising a dispute, releasing an accrual, submitting a line form.
