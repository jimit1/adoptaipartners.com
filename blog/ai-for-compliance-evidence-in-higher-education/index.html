AI for Compliance Evidence in Higher Education: Assembling the Proof Before Anyone Asks For It

25 September 2026  ·  8 minute read  ·  Adopt AI Partners

A university does not have a compliance function. It has many, none of them in the same building.

Evidence lives with academic departments, the research office, estates, admissions, the international team, student services and the registry. Each holds part of the proof and none holds all of it. So every time somebody external asks a precise question, a small group assemble the answer by hand, against a deadline somebody else set, and the cost falls on the people who can least afford it.

Evidence is continuous, assembly is episodic

The work being automated here is not judgement. It is locating evidence that already exists, mapping an obligation to the artefacts that prove it, noticing the gap while there is time to close it, and drafting a pack where every claim points at its source.

That is a retrieval and reconciliation problem across systems never designed to answer questions about each other: the student record system, finance, human resources, the research information system, the document store, a committee minute in a shared drive and a spreadsheet somebody keeps because no system does that bit. An agent suits it because it holds the whole map at once and can be made to say something is missing rather than offer the nearest thing to hand.

Periodic review and professional body accreditation

A review panel reads a claim the department made about itself and asks to see what supports it.

The claim in the submission and the artefact behind it

The submission says the programme responded to student feedback. The panel asks where. The honest answer is a survey result, a committee minute recording the discussion, an action against a named person, an approved module modification, and the descriptor version showing the change landed. The chain is only evidence if every link is intact.

An agent builds that chain per claim. It reads the draft submission, extracts every assertion needing support, retrieves candidate evidence, and returns each claim as supported with citations, partially supported with the weak link named, or unsupported. The last state is the valuable one, because it arrives while the department can still act on it.

The standing index rather than the scramble before a visit

Most institutions rebuild this map every review, then lose it. Held as a standing index, it turns the opening question of a cycle into which parts have decayed.

Answering the question a panel asks in the room

Panels ask unplanned questions. Show us the external examiner response for that module. An agent with read access retrieves the document and its provenance while the chair is still speaking. A person in the room reads it first.

The statutory return and the data quality behind it

Mechanically, a return is reconciliation. The difficulty is never the submission but the months of quiet data quality work that decide whether it holds up when somebody queries a figure.

An agent runs the validation rules continuously against live data rather than once at the deadline, so failing records surface while the people who can correct them still remember the case. It explains every material change against the previous submission in terms of the underlying records, and keeps the trail of amendments behind each reported figure. The job is to make the return understandable to the person who signs it.

Post award evidence and the funder audit file

An award is a set of conditions attached to money, and the proof sits across payroll, purchasing, timesheets and the academic's own records.

An agent maintains the audit file continuously rather than reconstructing it when a funder gives notice. It ties each charge to the budget line and the award condition permitting it, links claimed effort to the timesheet behind it, checks that certified effort matches recorded effort, and flags costs that look ineligible under the terms of that award.

The findings worth having are the cost with no supporting artefact and the artefact that does not say what the claim says. Both are cheap to fix in the month they occur and expensive afterwards. Certification stays with the named person who makes it.

Sponsorship duties and the engagement record

Duties attached to sponsoring international students are continuous, evidenced, and carry consequences for the student as well as the institution. The question is rarely whether engagement data exists. It is whether the data reflects what happened, whether the required checks were recorded at the right point, and whether an event carrying a reporting duty was recognised when it occurred rather than at the next audit.

An agent reconciles engagement and attendance records across the sources that generate them, identifies students whose records contradict themselves, and surfaces events with a reporting clock attached as soon as the data shows them, then drafts the supporting file. The report, and any decision affecting a student's status, belongs to a named person, because it touches their right to remain.

Health and safety across laboratories, workshops and fieldwork

Risk assessments here are written by academics and technicians, expert in the work and amateur at the paperwork. The assessment usually exists. What breaks is the link to everything that gives it force.

An agent holds the connection between an activity, the current assessment covering it, the named supervisor, the training records of the people who did the work, and the equipment. It reports assessments past their review date, work done by people with no recorded training for it, and fieldwork approved without the assessment your procedure requires. Risk itself is judged by the safety professionals.

Records where assembly is the only safe role

Some files can be assembled by an agent and must never be judged by one. That line belongs in the design, not in training.

Safeguarding and student protection

Assembly here means chronology and completeness: what was reported, when, to whom, what action followed and whether those actions were completed. It does not mean summarising a disclosure or characterising a concern. Access control on that file matters more than anywhere else here.

Complaints, appeals and misconduct files

An agent can gather the submission and evidence, build a chronology, retrieve the record and any support arrangements, state which published ground is engaged and what evidence speaks to it, and flag a stage skipped or a deadline missed. The outcome, and the reasoning recorded for it, belongs to the panel or named officer, who must be able to explain the decision from the record without leaning on what the system indicated.

Data protection records and proving a right was honoured

The record of processing activities is the document most likely to describe an institution that no longer exists, because systems are retired, new ones arrive through departmental purchases, and it is refreshed annually if at all.

An agent compares it against observable reality: the asset register, processor contracts, data flows to partners. It reports processing with no entry, entries describing systems nobody uses, and retention schedules the storage contradicts.

Rights requests are more useful. Recording that one was completed is easy. Evidencing that every system holding that person's data was searched and acted on is not. An agent reconstructs the search and names the systems it could not reach.

Programme approval, modification and what was actually taught

The approval file describes what was approved. The learning environment and the assessment records describe what happened. The evidence sits in the difference.

An agent compares the approved specification against delivery as recorded for a cohort: credit and assessment structure, outcomes assessed, modules offered and modification approvals in force. Where delivery diverged, it reports whether an approval exists for the divergence. Drift is normal and often pedagogically right. Undocumented drift is the finding.

Research ethics approvals and the amendments that follow

Approval is granted for a described study, and studies change. Amendments get agreed in principle and not always recorded against the approval.

An agent tracks approvals against outputs, data collection and participant facing materials, flagging activity that appears to sit outside the approved scope, approvals lapsed while work continues, and studies where the materials in use are not the approved versions. The committee retains every judgement.

Provision delivered by a partner in your name

Where another organisation delivers your award, you own the standards and someone else owns the records. Evidence arrives late, in their format, in their terms.

An agent normalises what partners send against what your agreement requires, reports each partner's evidence as complete, overdue or inconsistent, and reconciles their student and assessment data against your own. Comparing partners against each other is how a quiet problem surfaces before it becomes public.

Where AI fails here

Some of this work we would refuse to build.

The record is the first limit. If your obligation register is wrong, an agent files good evidence confidently against the wrong obligation, at scale, with a tidy citation trail. Neatness is persuasive. The map of obligation to owner has to be built with your people and allowed to return unresolved.

The second is the missing artefact. If something does not exist, the honest output is that it does not exist, not an inference about what probably happened and not an adjacent document offered in its place. Smoothing a gap manufactures false assurance in the one setting where assurance is the point.

The third is judgement. Whether the institution is compliant, whether an event is reportable, whether a standard was met, whether a student progresses: those decisions have names attached and the names stay attached. Anything touching progression, immigration status, safeguarding or disclosed disability belongs to a person who can be questioned about it.

Practical failure matters too. Document quality here is poor, so accuracy on clean files tells you nothing about the messy tail. We measure against contested cases from your own archive. And a tidy pack over a weak quality culture is worse than an untidy honest one, because it hides the real problem. If early output shows the work is not being done, that is the finding, not a paperwork task.

Starting with one review cycle

Take one department entering periodic review or professional body accreditation and run the agent alongside the people already doing it. That scope is small enough to supervise, has a fixed end date, and produces a comparison you can read: what the agent found, what your staff found, and what only one of them saw.

Then keep the index it built. The value compounds in the next cycle, when the question stops being where the evidence is.
