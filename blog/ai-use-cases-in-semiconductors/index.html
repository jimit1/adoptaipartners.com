AI Use Cases in Semiconductors: The Paperwork Around the Silicon

2 September 2026  ·  8 minute read  ·  Adopt AI Partners

Semiconductor companies are unusually good at hard technical problems and unusually tolerant of administrative ones. A fabless designer will spend a year closing timing on a critical block, then lose three weeks of engineering time assembling a qualification pack out of PDFs, spreadsheets and email threads. An OSAT will hold a lot over a documentation question nobody can answer quickly, because the answer sits in a change notice issued eleven months ago. A procurement team will run a multi-million dollar allocation from a spreadsheet one person maintains by hand.

This work is not glamorous and it is not going away. It is also, increasingly, work that AI agents can carry. Not the judgement, not the disposition, not the commitment of supply. The assembly, the cross-referencing, the drafting, the chasing.

What this article is not about

Two of the most sophisticated applications of machine learning anywhere in industry live inside this sector. Fab process control, including virtual metrology, defect classification from inspection images and yield learning, is a mature specialist field with its own vendors, data infrastructure and PhDs. So is design automation, where the major EDA suppliers have been shipping machine learning into place and route, verification coverage and analogue sizing for years.

Neither is what Adopt AI Partners does, and neither is what this article covers. If you want better defect classification on an inspection tool, buy it from the people who build inspection tools. What follows is the other side of the house: the document-heavy and coordination-heavy work that surrounds the silicon and that nobody has properly automated, because it never looked like a technology problem.

The real bottleneck is assembly, not analysis

Look closely at the tasks that eat your operations, quality, supply chain and finance teams and most share a shape. The information already exists, in your PLM, your ERP, your quality system, your test databases, your supplier portals and a shared drive that four people understand. The cost is in finding it, reconciling sources that disagree, formatting it into whatever the recipient demands, and chasing the two fields nobody filled in.

That is the shape of work an agent handles well, and the shape traditional automation never touched, because every customer wants a different template and every supplier sends a different format.

Supplier and foundry documentation

Foundry and OSAT relationships generate a continuous stream of documents: process design kits and their release notes, qualification reports, reliability data, capacity confirmations, engineering notices, scorecards. Most of it arrives as PDF or portal downloads and is read once by one person.

An agent can monitor those inbound channels, extract the substance, and route it. A PDK release note gets summarised against the versions your active projects are built on, so programme managers see which tape-outs are affected rather than being told a release happened. Reliability reports get parsed into structured fields and filed against the process and package they belong to, so the next time a customer asks for HTOL data you retrieve it instead of reconstructing it. Nothing is decided by the agent. It reads, extracts, links and flags.

PCN and ECN change notice handling

Product change notices are the clearest case in the whole sector. An inbound PCN starts a clock. Someone has to determine which of your parts, assemblies and customer programmes are affected, whether requalification is required, what the last time buy position is, and what you tell your own customers. Teams do this by hand, and the accuracy depends on who happened to pick it up.

An agent reads the incoming notice, extracts the affected part numbers, effective dates and change class, and cross-references your bill of materials and approved vendor list to produce the impact list. It drafts the internal assessment in your template with the affected programmes named and the requalification question posed, and drafts the outbound customer notification where one is owed. Your component engineer decides whether requalification is needed. The agent does the cross-referencing that used to precede that decision.

The same applies outbound. When you issue a PCN or an ECN, an agent can assemble the distribution list from your contract and quality agreement records, draft the notice in each customer's required format, track acknowledgements, and chase the ones outstanding.

Quality and yield report assembly

Monthly and quarterly quality reporting is a recurring assembly job. Yield by product and lot, test escape rates, DPPM against customer targets, outlier lots, open corrective actions, commentary. The analysis is yours. The gathering is mechanical.

An agent pulls the numbers from the test and yield databases, builds the report in the standard format, populates the trend commentary with what actually changed since last period, and marks values that moved outside your control limits for a human to explain. Customer scorecards, which nearly always want the same data cut a different way, come from the same underlying pull rather than from four people rebuilding four spreadsheets.

RMA and customer quality complaint triage

A complaint arrives with a part number, a lot code, a symptom description and often a photograph. Before anyone can respond, someone has to confirm the part is yours, trace the lot back through assembly and test, check whether related complaints exist, confirm the contractual position, and open the record in the right system.

An agent does that trace on arrival. It resolves the lot to its wafer, assembly site and test history, pulls the datalog summary, searches for prior complaints with the same signature, checks the shipping records, and opens a triage summary with all of it attached. The failure analysis engineer starts with evidence rather than a search. Where the complaint duplicates a known and closed issue, the agent drafts the response and links the prior 8D, and a person sends it.

Export control and compliance paperwork

Export classification, licence tracking, end-use documentation and restricted party screening are unforgiving and largely clerical. The risk is not that people cannot do the work. It is that the volume causes corners to be cut on a Friday afternoon.

Agents fit the preparation layer. They assemble the classification file for a new part by gathering the technical parameters that drive the determination and presenting them against the relevant control text. They pre-screen order and customer records and flag matches for review. They monitor licence conditions and expiry dates. They draft end-use statements and supporting documentation packs. The determination itself stays with your trade compliance function, documented and signed by a person, because that is both the legal expectation and the sensible one.

Long-lead procurement and allocation admin

Buyers in this industry spend a large share of their week on administration rather than negotiation: chasing acknowledgements, reconciling supplier commit dates against the plan, updating the allocation sheet, answering internal questions about where a part sits, preparing the shortage review.

An agent reconciles supplier confirmations against open purchase orders daily and reports only the deviations. It drafts expedite and reschedule requests. It maintains the allocation view from live data instead of from one person's spreadsheet, and prepares the shortage meeting pack with the movements since last week already highlighted. Who gets the constrained parts is a commercial decision and stays with your team.

Datasheets and product change documentation

Datasheets, errata, application notes and product change documentation drift out of alignment with the silicon and with each other. Keeping them aligned is unpopular work because it is detailed and invisible when done well.

An agent compares the datasheet against current characterisation data and flags parameters where the published limits no longer match what the product demonstrates. When a change is approved, it identifies every document referencing the affected parameter or package and drafts the revisions with changes marked, routed into your existing document control workflow. It never releases a controlled document. It removes the reason releases get delayed.

Customer qualification packs

Winning designs at automotive, industrial and hyperscale customers means producing qualification packs: reliability data, PPAP or equivalent, process flow and control plans, FMEA references, change history, site certifications, material declarations. Each customer wants its own structure. Most of the content already exists from previous submissions.

An agent maintains the underlying evidence library, maps each customer's required structure to it, assembles the pack, and produces a gap list of what genuinely does not exist yet. Your quality engineers close the gaps and review the submission rather than hunting for a certificate issued three years ago. On repeat submissions the saving is largest, because the agent knows what was sent last time and what has changed since.

Where to start

Choose the case where the information already exists in systems you trust, the output is a draft rather than a decision, and the cycle repeats often enough to show a result within a quarter. In most semiconductor firms that means inbound PCN impact assessment, RMA triage, or qualification pack assembly.

Scope it to one product family or one customer. Write down, before anything is built, what the agent may read, what it may write back, and what always goes to a named person. Measure something leadership already tracks: days to close a PCN assessment, hours per qualification pack, RMA acknowledgement time.

Where AI fails here

It fails when the source data is wrong. Stale approved vendor lists, bills of material that do not match what is actually built, and lot genealogy with gaps will produce confident and incorrect impact lists. Scope the agent to the systems you trust and fix the rest deliberately.

It has no place in process control or design signoff. Nothing described here belongs near an equipment recipe, a statistical process control loop or a tape-out gate. Those are separate disciplines with their own tools and validation regimes.

It fails on judgement carrying legal or contractual weight. Export classification, material disposition, requalification necessity and allocation between customers are decisions for accountable people. An agent allowed to conclude rather than prepare will eventually conclude something expensive.

It fails where confidentiality is not designed in from the start. Foundry PDKs, customer roadmaps and process detail are among the most tightly controlled information in any industry. A deployment has to satisfy your NDAs and your customers' security requirements before it does anything useful, and if that cannot be demonstrated the project should not proceed.

And it fails when it adds a system instead of removing work. If your engineers still open the same four tools afterwards, nothing has been gained.
