AI Use Cases in Legal Teams: Where an Agent Earns Its Place

31 August 2026  ·  8 minute read  ·  Adopt AI Partners

A legal team inside a large organisation runs two jobs at once. One is judgement: the advice, the negotiation, the call on risk that somebody has to own. The other is logistics. Finding the executed version. Working out who should look at this. Reading four hundred pages to answer one question about notice periods.

The second job consumes most of the week and produces none of the value people remember you for. That is where AI agents belong. Not as a substitute for a lawyer's opinion, but as the thing that does the reading, the locating, the first drafts and the tracking.

For each use case below the questions are the same: what does the agent read, what does it draft, and where does a qualified lawyer sign.

Getting work through the door

Contract intake and triage

Most legal teams do not have one contract queue. They have five: a shared mailbox, a chat channel, a form nobody fills in properly, direct messages, and procurement forwarding things at signature.

An intake agent reads whatever arrives. It identifies the document type and extracts the counterparty, the value, the requesting business unit and the deadline, then checks your contract records to see whether this is new paper, a renewal, an amendment or a duplicate of something already in flight.

It drafts the matter record: type, risk band under your own criteria, suggested owner, and a summary of what the requester wants. It replies asking for the two or three missing facts that always hold things up, such as the term, the data involved, or what has already been promised commercially.

A person confirms the risk band and assignment before work starts. The agent states no legal position to the business.

Matter intake and routing

The same pattern applies to advice requests, employment questions, marketing reviews and regulatory queries. The agent classifies the request against your matter taxonomy, checks whether a similar matter exists, pulls the relevant prior advice, and routes to the right specialist with a briefing note attached. Where an answer already exists in your guidance library, it drafts a response pointing there and a lawyer approves it.

The value is not the routing. It is that the specialist opens the matter knowing what was asked, what was answered last time and which documents are relevant.

Review, drafting and self service

Playbook based first pass and redlining

This gets the least space here, because we have written about it at length in a separate piece on AI for contract review. The short version: the agent reads third party paper, maps clauses against your playbook, records what the contract says, what your position requires and the gap between them, and proposes redlines only where an approved fallback exists. Everything requiring a judgement call goes to a lawyer as a flagged issue with the language quoted verbatim, never as a suggested edit. Read that piece for where the handover line sits.

NDA and low risk agreement self service

The prize here is removing legal from the path entirely for agreements that never needed a lawyer. The agent sits behind a request form the business uses directly. It takes the counterparty details and purpose, generates your standard mutual NDA, and issues it for signature under thresholds set in advance.

When the counterparty returns amendments, the agent compares them against your approved fallbacks. Changes inside them are accepted and logged. Anything outside stops, and the document goes to a lawyer with the deviation highlighted.

The lawyer's involvement moves from every NDA to the design of the thresholds, which is where your risk appetite is actually expressed and is not work to rush.

Living with what you signed

Obligation extraction and post signature management

Signature is where most contract processes stop and most contract risk begins. An obligation agent works through executed agreements and extracts what each party committed to: service levels, reporting deadlines, audit rights, insurance requirements, notice periods, price review mechanics, data handling duties, change of control.

Each obligation is stored with a citation to its clause, an owner in the business, a due date, and the consequence of missing it. The agent then monitors. It drafts the reminder before a deadline, drafts the notice letter where one is due, and flags where evidence of compliance was expected and never arrived.

A person owns every obligation. The agent never asserts an obligation has been met and never sends a contractual notice. It prepares the notice; a lawyer sends it.

Clause and precedent retrieval across a messy estate

Ask a large organisation where its contracts are and the answer involves several systems, a decade of shared drives and at least one acquisition nobody has fully absorbed. A retrieval agent indexes that estate as it is, rather than waiting for a migration. Asked whether any customer agreement contains an uncapped indemnity, or how the team has previously drafted a limitation of liability for a public sector buyer, it returns the actual clauses with document, version, date and counterparty attached. It shows the range of positions historically accepted, which beats a single model clause.

This is safe to delegate because it is verifiable. The agent locates and quotes; it does not interpret. The lawyer decides what the precedent means here.

Entity and corporate records upkeep

Company secretarial work is unglamorous and expensive to get wrong. An agent reads board minutes, resolutions, share registers, filing confirmations and registry extracts, and reconciles them against your entity management system. It flags directors recorded internally who no longer appear on the register, filings approaching a deadline, registered office details that disagree between systems, and subsidiaries with no minutes in a year.

It drafts the filings and standard resolutions. A company secretary or qualified adviser reviews and files. No agent submits to a registry.

Disputes, regulation and spend

Litigation hold and disclosure preparation

When a hold is triggered, the agent drafts the notice, builds the custodian list from your HR and systems records rather than from memory, and chases acknowledgements. It maintains the register and drafts the release notice when the matter closes.

On disclosure, it works within your review platform on the collected set, applying first pass relevance classifications and flagging documents likely to attract privilege for a reviewer to examine. It drafts chronologies and key document summaries with citations.

Privilege calls are made by lawyers, and so is any relevance decision that goes to the other side. The agent narrows the pile and records how it was narrowed, which is itself disclosable and has to be clean.

Regulatory horizon scanning turned into internal actions

Scanning alone produces a newsletter nobody reads. The useful version connects the change to your organisation. The agent monitors the sources your team already trusts, and for each relevant development drafts an assessment: what changed, which entities, products or processes it touches, and which internal policies and templates reference that area.

The output is a draft action item with an owner and a deadline, in the same system as the rest of your work. A lawyer confirms applicability before anything is circulated. Nobody in the business should receive a regulatory interpretation a qualified person has not approved.

Outside counsel spend and billing guideline review

Guidelines get breached routinely and nobody has time to check line by line. An agent reads every invoice against the engagement letter and the guidelines: rates against agreed rates, timekeepers against the approved list, block billing, travel, administrative time, duplicate attendance, work outside scope.

It drafts the challenge with the lines cited and a proposed adjustment, plus the variance note where a matter runs over budget. Legal operations decides what to raise, and the partner conversation stays human.

Where to start

Start with the queue where the inputs are already digital, the rules are written down, and a human review step already exists that you intend to keep. Contract intake and obligation extraction usually qualify. Litigation and regulatory interpretation usually do not, at least not first.

Run the agent alongside the current process for a few weeks and compare its output to your team's. Where it disagrees, tighten the escalation rules rather than the prompt.

Where AI fails here

Language models fabricate authority. They will produce a plausible citation to a case or provision that does not exist, or that exists and says something else. Never let an agent's output stand as a statement of law without a qualified person checking the source. Retrieval that quotes your own documents is safer than generation that recalls the world.

Privilege and confidentiality are structural problems, not settings. Sending privileged material through a system that retains or trains on it can put privilege at risk. Resolve that in the architecture and the provider contracts before a single matter touches the tool, and note it is a different question for a firm holding client data than for an in house team holding its own.

Professional responsibility does not delegate. A regulated professional remains accountable for advice under their name, and supervision has to be real. If your review step is a lawyer clicking approve on output they have not read, that is not a control, it is a liability.

Jurisdiction breaks these tools quietly. A model trained largely on one legal tradition applies its assumptions to a document governed by another, and the output reads fluently while being wrong. Multi jurisdiction estates need jurisdiction as an explicit input and a specialist reviewing the result.

Negotiation is judgement, not pattern matching. An agent can tell you a liability cap sits below your standard. It cannot tell you this counterparty will concede next week, or that this is the point worth losing to win another.

And the quietest failure: a summary accurate about the clause and silent about the carve out that guts it. Missing an exception is worse than saying nothing, because it produces confidence where a lawyer would have kept reading. This is why every extraction above carries a citation to the source text, and why sampling by qualified people does not stop after go live.
