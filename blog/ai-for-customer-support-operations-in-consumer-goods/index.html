AI for Customer Support Operations in Consumer Goods: The Care Line and the Complaint Behind It

2 October 2026  ·  8 minute read  ·  Adopt AI Partners

A branded manufacturer has two kinds of customer, and the single word covers both badly. One is the person who bought the pack, opened it at home and found something they did not expect. The other is the retailer who stocks it, whose buying, supply chain and accounts contacts all have questions of their own. They arrive through different doors and need different people.

Nearly all of the volume and nearly all of the risk sit on the consumer side, where a contact can be a compliment, a question about where to buy, a misreading of a competition rule, or the first line of a quality investigation that ends on the factory floor. The work is partly answering and largely classifying, because a contact sorted into the wrong queue is the one that causes harm. Everything below is about the consumer queue, apart from one section on routing a trade contact.

The doors a consumer arrives through

The care line printed on pack is still the front door for a large share of contacts, and the people who use it expect a human voice. Email and the website form bring longer accounts with photographs attached. Social channels bring the shortest messages with the largest audience, where a reply is a publication rather than a response.

The expectations of speed, tone and privacy differ sharply, and nothing specific to a person belongs in a public reply. An agent earns its place quietly here: reading a message in whatever form it arrives, assembling the product information, and drafting in the register the consumer chose. That classification work is what drifts when it is done by hand under pressure.

The same brand answering in more than one language

Many teams cover several markets from one room, and translation is the easy half. The answer about an ingredient, a claim or an offer is market specific, so an agent must retrieve against the correct market, never a neighbouring one.

When a contact stops being a service conversation

Some contacts are service conversations. Some are the opening of a technical file, and the difference needs recognising in the first exchange, not three replies later.

A usable complaint record needs the same things every time. What the consumer saw, in their own words, not summarised into a category too early. The product, down to pack format and size. The batch or date code from the pack. Where and roughly when it was bought. Whether any product is still in their possession, and whether they kept the packaging. Photographs of the product, the defect and the code panel. Whether anyone consumed or used it.

Collecting that is a structured interview, and the gaps are predictable: consumers rarely volunteer a batch code, often discard the packaging, and usually describe a symptom rather than a format. Asking for the missing pieces while the pack is still within reach beats a form nobody completes.

Reading the code off a photograph

Date and batch codes are inkjet printed on film, foil, glass or board, usually at an angle and in poor light. Automated reading helps, with one hard rule: a partly legible code must be recorded as partly legible. Reading "L3214 BB 09" when the fourth character could be something else sends the investigation to the wrong production window, and nobody downstream knows it was invented. Record what can be read, flag what is uncertain, keep the image attached, and ask the consumer to confirm.

Where the handover ends

A complete, correctly classified record then goes to quality with the photographs, the code, the retention status and the consumer's own words intact. The investigation, the corrective action and any traceability work that follow belong to the technical function and are not the subject of this page.

Illness, injury and the contact that must stop

A small number of contacts describe someone who became unwell, had a reaction, or was hurt. These are not a category to be handled faster. They are a stop condition.

The only safe automated behaviour is to recognise the signal early, capture carefully, reassure nobody about causation, and escalate at once to a named person with authority to act. Never a view on cause, never a hint that the symptoms are unrelated, never a closed contact. Test what happens when one arrives in the evening, because one will.

Allergen and ingredient questions answered from the specification

Someone is standing in a kitchen deciding whether to give the product to a child with an allergy. A near enough answer is a harm, not an inconvenience.

The answer has to come from the current specification for that pack format in that market, including precautionary labelling and any recent recipe change. Not from a general web page, older artwork, or a confident paraphrase of a similar product in the range. Build in the rule that when the specification cannot be retrieved, the agent says so and routes to a person. Silence is survivable. An invented answer is not.

Where to buy it, and the line nobody mentioned

A steady stream of contacts is people trying to find the product: a format that moved aisle, a size a retailer stopped listing, a pack whose appearance changed. Inside it sits the discontinued line nobody told care about, where a loyal consumer asks where it has gone and the answer lands publicly and wrong. An agent is only as good as the range data it reads, so current status for every active and withdrawn line, in one place care can query, is worth having anyway.

On pack offers arrive as a wave

Competitions, cashback, collector mechanics and money off promotions produce a surge you can see coming, because you set the dates. They also produce a predictable set of problems: a code that will not validate, a receipt rejected, a pack bought in a market the offer excludes, and the consumer who read the terms differently from the person who wrote them.

That last one is the real work, because terms are written quickly and read literally. An agent grounded on the mechanic and its exclusions handles the straightforward volume and shows which clause is being misread while the promotion is live and the wording can still be clarified.

Vouchers, replacements and the authority behind a yes

Care teams spend real money closing conversations: vouchers, replacement product, postage, the occasional refund. The amount is rarely the problem. The inconsistency is.

Two consumers with the same experience should get the same gesture, in a quiet week or the busiest week of a launch, from an experienced adviser or a new one. A firm policy applied consistently costs less than a generous one applied at random. Automation helps by making the ceiling explicit, limiting the agent to approved remedies, and escalating as soon as the value or a pattern of repeat contacts crosses a boundary.

Seasonal lines, launches and a team that grows for a few weeks

A seasonal range, a campaign going live, a new format landing in a major account or an unflattering piece of coverage will all move volume sharply within a day. The temptation is to widen automation just before the spike. The better use of the same effort is to make a temporarily larger team accurate: retrieval that hands a new colleague the right answer with its source attached, drafts they review before sending, and context assembled on every escalation. Someone who joined this week can send a correct reply if the system does the retrieval. What they cannot do is know which of last season's exceptions still applies.

What the care line hears first

A handful of contacts about one line, from one region, inside a short period is often the earliest signal anywhere in the business that something changed on a production line or a distribution route. It reaches quality and supply later than it should, because the contacts sit in free text, categorised at speed by people whose priority was the next call. Consistent classification of closed contacts, by line, pack format, defect type, market and retailer, turns the queue into an instrument, which is often worth more than the handling time saved.

Trade contacts that belong on another desk

Retailer and distributor contacts land on the consumer line constantly: a chase on an order, a short delivery, a query about a claim. Recognising one is easy to automate and genuinely useful. Resolving one is not care's job, because the systems, the evidence and the commercial authority sit elsewhere. Route it with the references and attachments carried across, and treat accurate routing as the whole contribution.

Where AI fails here

The pack is the evidence and only the consumer has it. Once the packaging is in the bin, no technology recovers the code.

Image reading is fallible. Codes on crushed film, condensation or dark glass defeat it, and a system that guesses rather than flagging uncertainty is worse than no reading at all.

Distress and vulnerability are not classification problems. A parent describing a reaction, an older consumer who has phoned for years, a person for whom this product is a dietary necessity: none will necessarily say so, and their words may sound routine.

Specifications that are not current defeat grounding. If recipe and artwork changes do not reach the source the agent reads, it answers with yesterday's formulation and complete confidence.

Nothing holds without an owner. Ranges, offers and policies move, and the care line is where a stale system becomes visible to the public.

One complaint type, from first contact to the quality file

Pick a single, well understood defect type on one line. The agent recognises the defect family, confirms product and pack format, asks for the code panel photograph and reads it, flags uncertain characters, establishes whether product and packaging are retained, captures what the consumer saw in their own words, checks explicitly for any report of illness or injury and stops dead if there is one, applies the goodwill rule within its ceiling, and hands quality a complete record with the images attached.

Measure two things. How often quality receives a file it can act on without going back to the consumer, and how long the consumer waited for a sensible reply. If both improve on one defect type, the pattern extends to the next. If they do not, you have found your data gaps.
